# Delegate the diary, don't clone Fitatu

Coolinary can't officially push its meals into Fitatu, Yazio, MyFitnessPal or Lifesum, but it can read what users log in them, and that difference should set the strategy. Fitatu has no public or partner API, its terms ban scripted access, and none of the major trackers imports nutrition that another app writes to Apple Health or Health Connect; FatSecret is the only tracker with a self-serve API that writes into a user's diary. The other direction is open: every major tracker writes nutrition to the phone's health store (Fitatu only for Premium subscribers), so for an estimated **1.5–3 developer-weeks** Coolinary can read "already eaten today" from any of them, record meals the user confirms as eaten, and hand users an ingredient list to log elsewhere, all while remaining "not a food diary". An in-house tracker is a different order of cost: an estimated **9–13 person-weeks** for a lite "log what you ate from the plan" version and **33–50 person-weeks (8–12 months solo)** for a Fitatu-style clone, which would still launch with a Polish food database about 5% the size of Fitatu's, require GDPR Article 9 consent and a DPIA, break three of Coolinary's own rules, and compete with Fitatu in Poland and Yazio in Germany in a category where most users stop logging within 4–12 weeks. Coolinary's price already sits at tracker level, so a diary cannot raise it, and no public data shows that adding a diary to a meal planner lifts conversion or retention. The recommendation is to ship copy-to-tracker helpers at launch, build the health-store link in the release that introduces calorie goals, build a lite in-app log only if launch data ask for it, and not build a full clone.

## Fitatu and the other big trackers accept no meals from outside apps

Fitatu offers nothing for Coolinary to connect to. Its only published partnership channel is a marketing inbox for influencers, food producers, ad buyers and event sponsors, with no mention of an API, integrations or technology partners ([Fitatu help](https://www.fitatu.com/pl/help/jakie-sa-mozliwosci-wspolpracy-z-fitatu)). The one working client, the open-source `fitatu-api`, was reverse-engineered from Fitatu's web and Android apps and can read and write the diary, goals, weight and private recipes ([GitHub fitatu_library](https://github.com/Jezue/fitatu_library)), so the link is technically possible. It is not legal: **Fitatu's terms ban automated access by bots or scripts (§VI.4(a)), using its data for anything other than Fitatu's services (§IX.3), and letting third parties use an account (§IV.6)** ([Fitatu Regulamin](https://www.fitatu.com/pl/regulations?p=959)), and the EU Court of Justice's *Ryanair v PR Aviation* ruling lets database owners enforce such limits by contract ([Kluwer Copyright Blog](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/ryanair-ltd-v-pr-aviation-bv-contracts-rights-and-users-in-a-low-cost-database-law/)). A Coolinary server holding users' Fitatu passwords would add custody of another company's credentials and Article 9 health data to the terms breach, and Fitatu could cut it off overnight by rotating its client keys. Hobby tools such as VikFit, which copies Kuchnia Vikinga catering meals into Fitatu, prove that Polish users want this bridge, but VikFit itself calls the tool unofficial and warns it may stop working ([vikfit.pl](https://vikfit.pl/)).

What remains legitimate is narrow. Fitatu's iOS universal-link file shows the app opens `fitatu.com` links to search, recipes, products and its text, voice and photo AI estimation screens ([Fitatu AASA file](https://www.fitatu.com/.well-known/apple-app-site-association)), so Coolinary can send a user to a Fitatu screen but cannot create content through a link. The most practical push today is a **"Copy for Fitatu" button**: Coolinary copies the person's portion as an ingredient list in grams, the user pastes it into Fitatu's text-AI estimation, and Fitatu matches it against its own database. It needs nobody's permission, but it costs two or three taps and works only for **Premium+AI subscribers, about 150,000 people** ([XYZ.pl](https://xyz.pl/tworcy-sympatii-oddali-kontrole-w-fitatu-grupa-znanych-inwestorow-rozkreci-zywieniowa-aplikacje-w-europie-zachodniej/)). Fitatu also sells a paid "recipe database integration" that places an advertiser's recipes permanently in its catalogue, priced on request ([Cityboard](https://cityboard.pl/blog/713-reklama-na-fitatu)), but that product is built for food brands buying placement, not for thousands of recipes scaled to each person. Two further cautions apply. Any recipe a user saves into Fitatu becomes material Fitatu may use, sublicense and **use to train AI models** under §XIV.15–17 of its terms ([Fitatu Regulamin](https://www.fitatu.com/pl/regulations?p=959)). And Fitatu already sells a Premium "Lodówka" (fridge) feature that turns typed ingredients into catalogue recipes ([Fitatu blog](https://www.fitatu.com/blog/en/not-sure-what-to-do-with-the-leftovers-in-your-fridge-take-advantage-of-the-fitatu-fridge-and-treat-yourself-to-a-meal/)), which gives it a commercial reason not to open its diary to a fridge-to-meals app. A short e-mail to wspolpraca@fitatu.com costs nothing, but the realistic reply is a co-marketing offer like Fitatu's January 2026 Premium discount for Lidl Plus users ([Fitatu blog](https://www.fitatu.com/blog/nowy-rok-nowe-cele-zrob-pierwszy-krok-do-formy-z-fitatu-i-lidl-plus/)), not API access.

The other trackers are mostly as closed. MyFitnessPal's API site says it is "not accepting requests for API access at this time" ([myfitnesspalapi.com](https://www.myfitnesspalapi.com/)). Yazio has no public API, lists one as "under consideration" on its own feature board ([Yazio feature board](https://yazioen.featureupvote.com/suggestions/28294/expose-public-api)), and offers only an affiliate programme ([Yazio partners](https://www.yazio.com/en/become-a-partner)). Cronometer shares its API only with practitioner platforms ([Cronometer Pro FAQ](https://cronometer.com/blog/cronometer-pro-faq)), and Lose It!'s partner API is in beta and "read-only" ([Lose It! API](https://d2qjq4b9ehc3ag.cloudfront.net/api)). **FatSecret is the exception: its self-serve Platform API includes `food_entry.create`, which writes to a user's food diary through three-legged OAuth** ([FatSecret docs](https://platform.fatsecret.com/docs/v1/food_entry.create)), and its Premier Free edition costs nothing for start-ups with under US$1M in revenue and under US$1M raised ([FatSecret API editions](https://platform.fatsecret.com/api-editions)). The catches are that Premier Free covers only the US dataset, Polish and German localisation is offered to "select accounts" ([FatSecret localization](https://platform.fatsecret.com/docs/guides/localization)), every entry needs a FatSecret food and serving ID, and the docs do not say whether API-created entries appear in the consumer app. FatSecret still matters in Poland, where a 2026 guide ranks it the second free tracker after Fitatu ([Komputronik](https://nano.komputronik.pl/n/aplikacje-do-liczenia-kalorii-najlepsze-kalkulatory-kalorii-na-smartfon/)). Samsung Health's Data SDK can also write nutrition, but only after Samsung approves a partner request ([Samsung Health Data SDK FAQ](https://developer.samsung.com/health/data/faq.html)).

Public recipe pages are a cheap partial route. MyFitnessPal imports recipes from a link only on its English-language website ([MFP support](https://support.myfitnesspal.com/hc/en-us/articles/360032271592-How-does-the-Recipe-Importer-on-the-website-work)), Cronometer's importer is for Gold subscribers only ([Cronometer blog](https://cronometer.com/blog/recipe-importer/)), and MacroFactor imports name, servings and ingredients from a link ([MacroFactor help](https://help.macrofactorapp.com/en/articles/259-import-recipes-from-link)). Yazio users have asked for link import without getting it ([Yazio feature board](https://yazioen.featureupvote.com/suggestions/90212/import-recipes-from-web-by-entering-the-url)), and no import was found in Fitatu or Lifesum. Publishing each Coolinary recipe as a public page with schema.org markup and gram weights helps search rankings and reaches those three importers, but every tracker recalculates nutrition from its own database, so its total will not match Coolinary's "about 520 kcal".

| Tracker | Official write into its diary | Official read of intake | Imports nutrition other apps write to Apple Health / Health Connect | Recipe import from a link | Realistic route for Coolinary |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fitatu | None | None (Health export is Premium-only) | No: reads activity, burned kcal, weight | No | Read via the phone's health store; "Copy for Fitatu"; deep links; co-marketing |
| Yazio | None | None | No: reads activity and weight | No (requested) | Read via the health store; affiliate only |
| MyFitnessPal | Closed | Closed | No | English website only | Read via the health store; schema.org pages |
| Lifesum | None | None | No | Not found | Read via the health store |
| Cronometer | Selected partners only | Selected partners only | No | Gold only | Read via the health store; niche in PL/DE |
| FatSecret | **Yes, self-serve API** | Yes, API | No | Not found | Prototype on Premier Free; ask for PL/DE data |
| Lose It! | No | Read-only partner beta | **Yes, food by food (iOS)** | Unclear | Writes to the health store reach it; small in PL/DE |
| MacroFactor | None | None | **Daily totals only** | Yes | Writes reach days with no MacroFactor log |
| Samsung Health | Only with an approved partnership | With registration | n/a | n/a | Reach it through Health Connect |

## Health stores make every tracker a data source, not a destination

The idea of writing Coolinary meals to Apple Health or Health Connect and letting Fitatu pick them up fails, because no major tracker reads food from the health store. Fitatu says its integrations "send data on meals, weight, and height" and receive only burned calories, workouts and activity ([Fitatu help](https://www.fitatu.com/en/help/can-fitatu-sync-with-other-devices-and-apps)). MyFitnessPal syncs food from MyFitnessPal to Apple Health only ([MFP help](https://support.myfitnesspal.com/hc/en-us/articles/360032271092-Apple-Health-connection-and-syncing)); Lifesum imports exercise, steps and weight but no food ([Lifesum help](https://help.lifesum.com/nl/article/how-to-connect-to-and-sync-with-apple-health-ios-wosoup)); Cronometer staff say it does not import nutrient information ([Cronometer forum](https://forums.cronometer.com/discussion/6325/import-food-data-from-apple-health)); Yazio reads activity and weight ([Yazio help](https://help.yazio.com/hc/en-us/articles/360018833998-YAZIO-and-Apple-Health)); and FatSecret pulls only calories burned and steps ([FatSecret help](https://www.fatsecret.com/fatsecret-app-help/third-party-apps/apple-health-data-sync-with-fatsecret)). Only **Lose It!**, which shows foods from Apple Health in its log with a heart icon ([Lose It! help](https://loseit.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/47773580085140-Using-Apple-Health-With-Lose-It)), and **MacroFactor**, which imports daily totals only on days the user logged nothing in MacroFactor ([MacroFactor 2.9.3](https://macrofactor.com/version-2-9-3/); [MacroFactor help](https://help.macrofactorapp.com/settings/data_sources/)), would show Coolinary-written meals, and both are small in Poland and Germany.

Read the other way, the same list is the opportunity. Because Fitatu, Yazio, MyFitnessPal, Lifesum, Cronometer and FatSecret all *write* nutrition to the health stores, **Coolinary can read "already eaten today" without integrating with any tracker**, and use it to size the phone owner's remaining meals to their target minus what they have eaten. The biggest caveat is in Poland: Fitatu sends data to Apple Health and Health Connect only on Premium ([Fitatu Regulamin §III.1](https://www.fitatu.com/pl/regulations?p=959)), which covers **about 240,000 payers out of roughly 1.2 million monthly users, or about 20%** ([XYZ.pl](https://xyz.pl/tworcy-sympatii-oddali-kontrole-w-fitatu-grupa-znanych-inwestorow-rozkreci-zywieniowa-aplikacje-w-europie-zachodniej/)), so free Fitatu users need a manual "I've already eaten about X kcal" fallback. MyFitnessPal sends meal summaries rather than single foods, does not sync timestamps to Apple Health, and syncs only "periodically throughout the day" ([MFP Health Connect help](https://support.myfitnesspal.com/hc/en-us/articles/10553948248973)). Fitatu's exact data types and level of detail are undocumented and need a hands-on test. Double counting is a real risk, which is why Fitatu itself advises using "only one sync path": Coolinary should exclude its own records by each record's source field and ask the user to name "my tracker", and when that tracker is Fitatu or Yazio it should only read, never also write the user's eaten meals, because the user will log them in the tracker. HealthKit holds one person's data, so the link serves only the phone owner, never other family members.

The platform details are workable. HealthKit has 39 dietary data types and a `food` grouping with a free-text food-type field but **no meal-type field** ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/nutrition-type-identifiers)), and when a user denies read access the app simply sees no data ([Apple privacy](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/protecting-user-privacy)), so the interface should say "0 kcal found" rather than imply nothing was eaten. Health Connect's `NutritionRecord` carries both a dish name and a meal type ([Android reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/kotlin/androidx/health/connect/client/records/NutritionRecord)) and by default lets an app read other apps' data from 30 days before the first permission grant ([Android read data](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/guides/health-connect/develop/read-data)), so today's intake needs no history permission and can be read when the app opens. Health Connect is built into Android 14 and later, and Google Fit's APIs are supported only until the end of 2026 ([Android publish](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/publish)), so Health Connect is the only Android target. With the Flutter `health` plugin, which offers `writeMeal` and a nutrition type on both platforms ([pub.dev](https://pub.dev/packages/health)), or the equivalent React Native libraries, the research team estimates the whole link at **1.5–3 developer-weeks**: about a week for reading and edge cases, half a week for writing eaten meals, and half a week for store compliance, plus Google's review, which has no official deadline.

Compliance shapes the design more than code does. Apple's guideline 5.1.3(ii) bans writing "false or inaccurate data" into HealthKit and storing personal health information in iCloud ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)), so Coolinary should write only after an explicit "I ate this" with the user's own portion, never planned meals or other family members' portions. Eat This Much already works this way: syncing is Premium-only, opt-in and off by default, and exports only meals marked "I ate this" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/eat-this-much-meal-planner/id981637806)). Apple also bans HealthKit data from advertising and restricts disclosure to third parties ([Apple privacy](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/protecting-user-privacy)), and guideline 5.1.2(i) requires explicit permission before sharing personal data with third-party AI. The phone should therefore subtract eaten calories from the target and send only the remaining meal budget to the server or the AI, which matches Coolinary's current design where only the daily target leaves the phone. On Google Play, the Health apps declaration must justify each data type; "tracking nutrition" and "providing personalized… meal plans" are permitted uses, and health data may not be used for any advertising ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12991134)). Under GDPR, the EU data-protection authorities' 2015 guidance says apps that track food to lose weight most likely need explicit consent ([WP29 annex](https://ec.europa.eu/justice/article-29/documentation/other-document/files/2015/20150205_letter_art29wp_ec_health_data_after_plenary_annex_en.pdf)), and reading from the phone's store also falls under Article 5(3) of the ePrivacy Directive ([EDPB Guidelines 2/2023](https://edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-10/edpb_guidelines_202302_technical_scope_art_53_eprivacydirective_v2_en_0.pdf)). Keeping the data on the phone cuts the risk but does not remove the duty, so the link needs an explicit in-app opt-in and a privacy-policy section, alongside the separate consent Coolinary already plans for goals.

There may be upside ahead. Apple has been reported to be planning a food diary inside the Health app, now expected in iOS 27 ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/04/30/ios-26-4-improved-apples-health-app-and-bigger-upgrades-are-coming-soon/)), and one vendor analysis expects HealthKit's developer interface to stay essentially unchanged ([Sahha](https://sahha.ai/blog/ios-27-health-fitness-developers/)); Apple has confirmed neither. If Apple ships a diary that writes standard dietary records, the same link would read it at no extra cost.

## A credible tracker needs 33–50 person-weeks and a database Coolinary can't buy

A credible tracker in 2026 is expected to offer a diary split by meal, fast search over a large local-language database, barcode logging, quick-add, custom foods and recipes, "copy yesterday", macro goals and a weight log. AI photo logging has become a premium tier at the leaders: Fitatu sells it only in Premium+AI ([Fitatu help](https://www.fitatu.com/pl/help/czym-jest-funkcja-%22szacowanie-kalorii-ai-ze-zdjecia%22)), and MyFitnessPal bought Cal AI ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/02/myfitnesspal-has-acquired-cal-ai-the-viral-calorie-app-built-by-teens/)). Coolinary's plan already includes a nutrition engine (Ciqual, BLS and USDA tables), per-person targets with a 1,200 kcal floor, barcode lookups and a "cooked it" button that reduces stock, which keeps the lite option cheap. The estimates below are the research team's bottom-up figures for one experienced cross-platform developer, not sourced benchmarks, and they will move once the real codebase exists.

| Layer | Lite: "eaten from the plan" | Full clone adds | Estimated effort (person-weeks) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diary core | Intake data model, daily totals against each person's target, edit, recents, copy yesterday | History calendar, weekly and monthly charts, nutrient detail | Lite 2.5–3.5; full +2–3 |
| Logging inputs | "Mark planned meal eaten" with portion multiplier; quick-add kcal; barcode to diary with serving picker | Household measures (łyżka, Esslöffel); custom foods from label photos | Lite 2.5–3.5; full +2.5–4 |
| Food search and data | Existing catalogue | Own search over Open Food Facts plus BLS/Ciqual plus crowd entries, with Polish and German stemming and typo tolerance; moderation tools | Full +5–8 |
| Body and activity | None | Weight through Apple Health / Health Connect; wearable activity calories | Full +3.5–5 |
| AI logging | None | Photo and voice logging (AI outputs foods and grams); test set of about 100 Polish and German meals | Full +4–6 |
| Engagement | One daily reminder | Water, reminders, streaks without shaming | Full +1.5–2 |
| Compliance and privacy | Article 9 consent, per-adult privacy, export and delete, DPIA, diary off on child profiles, eating-disorder safeguards, methodology page | Same, wider scope | Lite 2–3 |
| Ship | Offline queue, QA, store declarations | Localisation, hardening, tracker paywall through store purchases | Lite 1.5–3; full +3–5 |
| **Total** | | | **Lite ≈ 9–13; full ≈ 33–50 (8–12 months solo)** |

The binding constraint is data, not code. Fitatu claims over 500,000 Polish items ([Fitatu help](https://www.fitatu.com/pl/help/czy-fitatu-posiada-najwieksza-baze-polskich-produktow-i-potraw)), while a live count of Open Food Facts on 30 October 2026 found **38,498 products sold in Poland, of which 26,381 have complete nutrition facts** ([OFF API PL](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/api/v2/search?countries_tags=en:poland&page_size=1&fields=code); [OFF API PL complete](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/api/v2/search?countries_tags=en:poland&states_tags=en:nutrition-facts-completed&page_size=1&fields=code)), **about 5% of Fitatu's Polish count**. Germany looks far better, with 432,640 products and 253,191 complete ([OFF API DE](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/api/v2/search?countries_tags=en:germany&page_size=1&fields=code); [OFF API DE complete](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/api/v2/search?countries_tags=en:germany&states_tags=en:nutrition-facts-completed&page_size=1&fields=code)), roughly eleven times the Polish figure and a credible starting base. Open Food Facts is licensed under the ODbL, which allows commercial use but requires a derivative database used publicly to be shared under the same licence with a free machine-readable copy ([ODbL 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/)), so Coolinary's crowd catalogue must stay separate from it or be contributed back. Buying data does not close the gap. FatSecret's free start-up tier covers only US data, with Polish and German data quote-only ([FatSecret API editions](https://platform.fatsecret.com/api-editions)); Edamam charges $14–299 a month and states no EU coverage ([Edamam](https://developer.edamam.com/food-database-api)); and Passio's per-token pricing comes to an estimated €1,500 a month at 10,000 monthly users if 30% of them log ([Passio](https://www.passio.ai/cost-breakdown)). The research found no Polish commercial product-nutrition API, so a Polish tracker would need a "scan, photograph the label, submit" loop from day one and someone to moderate it.

AI logging is cheap but imprecise, and the imprecision matters for a family brand. General vision models estimating calories from a photo alone miss by **about 30–36% on average** and underestimate large portions ([PMC12513282](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12513282/)). A 2025 study of 195 dishes found average errors of **123 kcal from the photo alone and 53 kcal (13.9%) when the model also had the ingredient list with amounts** ([PMC12655113](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12655113/)). From list prices, a photo costs an estimated €0.0006 on Claude Haiku 5.5, €0.0027 on Gemini 2.5 Flash and €0.0125 on Claude Sonnet 5.5 ([Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing); [Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). A clone could keep the "AI never states a calorie number" rule only if the AI outputs foods and grams and the nutrition tables calculate the energy. The paywall carries its own risk: Apple pulled Cal AI from the App Store in April 2026 for bypassing in-app purchase ([Cult of Mac](https://www.cultofmac.com/news/why-app-store-pulled-cal-ai-app)).

Compliance grows with the feature. A per-person intake log next to a target calculated from weight is health data: since the *Lindenapotheke* judgment the EU Court reads "data concerning health" broadly, and Germany's Federal Court of Justice has confirmed that competitors can sue over GDPR breaches, which matters in Germany's culture of competitor warning letters ([Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2024/10/ecj-lindenapotheke); [Taylor Wessing on BGH](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2025/03/bgh-lindenapotheke)). A diary therefore needs explicit, separately revocable consent, a DPIA and a processing record, diaries private to each adult inside the shared household, and no diary on child profiles. The lifestyle positioning can stay outside EU medical-device rules as long as nothing claims to treat or monitor disease, because the MDCG 2019-11 guidance still hinges on intended purpose ([European Commission](https://health.ec.europa.eu/latest-updates/update-mdcg-2019-11-rev1-qualification-and-classification-software-regulation-eu-2017745-and-2025-06-17_en)). Apple's guideline 1.4.1 requires disclosed methodology for health accuracy claims, and developers report nutrition apps rejected for missing citations ([Apple forum](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/767340)); Google Play's Health apps declaration has its own nutrition and weight-management category ([Google Play Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14738291)); and the EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency duties began applying on 2 August 2026 ([Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-08-03-eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-take-effect-2-august-2026)). A full clone also collides with three of Coolinary's own rules, and with its child-safety design.

| Coolinary rule | What a full clone needs | How to keep the rule |
|---|---|---|
| "Not a food diary" | A daily intake log for each person | No way around it. Any tracker is a diary, so make it opt-in, per adult, private, with short retention |
| No weight history | A weight log and chart, standard in trackers | Read weight from Apple Health / Health Connect, which own the history; Coolinary keeps only the current value |
| AI never states a calorie number | Photo logging that outputs kcal | AI outputs foods and grams; the tables calculate kcal |
| Children never put on a diet | A diary for everyone | Diary and daily tallies switched off on child profiles |

Running costs are mostly people, not servers. The research team estimates the full clone's cash cost at **€100–1,000 a month at 10,000 monthly users**, depending mostly on AI photo use and any licensed database, against €20–150 for the lite version. Founder time on product moderation and support would grow from about **10–20 hours a month at 10,000 monthly users to 80–170 hours at 100,000**, which means hiring help. One-off costs add an estimated €1,000–3,000 for a lawyer to review consent and privacy and €400–1,600 for a dietitian review. For scale, MacroFactor started with **two engineers** ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/customers/macrofactor)) and Cal AI had **seven employees** when it was sold ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/02/myfitnesspal-has-acquired-cal-ai-the-viral-calorie-app-built-by-teens/)); both were tracker-first companies with more than one builder.

## A clone wouldn't pay for itself and could hurt the brand

Each launch market has one dominant tracker. In Poland, Fitatu has about 1.2 million monthly users and about 240,000 paying subscribers, and its product revenue grew 45% to about PLN 20M in 2025 at a profit ([XYZ.pl](https://xyz.pl/tworcy-sympatii-oddali-kontrole-w-fitatu-grupa-znanych-inwestorow-rozkreci-zywieniowa-aplikacje-w-europie-zachodniej/)); Sensor Tower estimates its peak weekly store revenue there at about $101K in Q4 2025, against $22.9K for Yazio ([Sensor Tower PL](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q4-unified-top-5-health-and-fitness-revenue-pl-600af518241bc16eb8dce802)). In Germany, Yazio was the top-earning Health & Fitness app in Q2 2025 with about 2 million weekly active users ([Sensor Tower DE](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q2-unified-top-5-health%20and%20fitness-revenue-de-600af518241bc16eb8dce802)), ranks #5 in the German App Store's Health & Fitness chart with 448,095 ratings ([App Store DE](https://apps.apple.com/de/app/yazio-kalorienz%C3%A4hler-fasten/id946099227)), and reports more than 100 million users and more than 120 staff ([Yazio factsheet](https://filecontent.yazio.com/press/factsheet-yazio-en.pdf)). Across Health & Fitness, **the top 10% of apps take 92.6% of revenue** ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/health-fitness-app-subscription-benchmarks/)). A solo clone would compete for users who are tied to years of history and a database they trust; Fitatu users logged almost 15 million meals in a single week of January 2026 ([XYZ.pl](https://xyz.pl/tworcy-sympatii-oddali-kontrole-w-fitatu-grupa-znanych-inwestorow-rozkreci-zywieniowa-aplikacje-w-europie-zachodniej/)).

A diary also cannot raise Coolinary's price. Tier A Plus at **€34.99 a year matches Yazio's common €35.90 annual plan** ([App Store DE](https://apps.apple.com/de/app/yazio-kalorienz%C3%A4hler-fasten/id946099227)), and Tier B at **€19.99 a year matches what Fitatu earns per payer**, since PLN 20M across 240,000 payers is about PLN 83, or €19–20 a year ([XYZ.pl](https://xyz.pl/tworcy-sympatii-oddali-kontrole-w-fitatu-grupa-znanych-inwestorow-rozkreci-zywieniowa-aplikacje-w-europie-zachodniej/)). A diary would have to earn its keep through more conversions or longer retention, and **no public data shows that adding a diary to a planner, or a planner to a diary, does either**. The market evidence points the other way. MyFitnessPal sells its meal planner as an extra on top of tracking, in Premium+ at $99.99 against $79.99 a year and only in English-speaking markets ([MFP blog](https://blog.myfitnesspal.com/myfitnesspal-meal-planner-what-to-know/)). Samsung Food made calorie tracking free in December 2025, treating it as something to give away rather than sell ([SamMobile](https://www.sammobile.com/news/samsung-food-update-massive-gift-free-users/)). And consolidation runs one way, with trackers buying planners: MyFitnessPal bought Intent's meal-planning technology ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/myfitnesspal-announces-acquisition-of-intent-revolutionizing-personalized-meal-planning-for-members-302374108.html)) and then Cal AI, and its owner is exploring a sale above $1 billion ([Reuters via KFGO](https://kfgo.com/2026/04/09/fitness-and-health-app-myfitnesspal-explores-sale-sources-say/)).

User behaviour weakens the case further. A 2026 review of 68 studies found that **adherence fell over time in every calorie-counting app**: among MyFitnessPal Premium users, the share logging all meals on at least four days a week fell **from 39% in week 1 to 8% in week 12 and 0% by week 24**, while simplified counting kept a **median of 97% of days logged against 49% for detailed counting** ([Dugas et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13085986/)). Another study puts median disengagement from diet self-monitoring at about ten weeks ([JMIR Formative Research](https://formative.jmir.org/2022/2/e33603)). A full diary would get a burst of use and then be abandoned, which makes it a poor engine for annual subscriptions, while a one-tap "I ate this" on a planned meal matches the simplified pattern people keep up.

Turning costs into payers makes the trade-off concrete. Using Coolinary's business model, which puts contribution at about €16 per payer per year and needs about 2,250 payers for €3,000 net a month, my calculation is that the full clone's estimated €100–1,000 a month at 10,000 monthly users, or €1,200–12,000 a year, would take **75–750 payers just to cover its running costs, between 3% and 33% of the whole break-even target**; the lite version's €20–150 a month takes 15–113 payers. Time is the larger cost. At 33–50 person-weeks the clone takes longer than the six-month window in which the business model's stop rule decides the company's fate (move to B2B or stop if install-to-paid stays under 1% and 30-day retention under 8%), so building it before launch would delay the first real test of the core idea by 8–12 months. AI cost tells the same story: an active photo logger costs about €0.6 a year on Claude Haiku 5.5 and about €2.4 on Gemini 2.5 Flash, but about €10.8 on Claude Sonnet 5.5, roughly three times Coolinary's €3.5 cap on AI and hosting per payer per year, so photo logging fits the budget only on small models and only for payers.

The brand risk falls hardest on a family product. Diet-app users report more disordered eating and body dissatisfaction than fitness-app users and non-users ([Psychology of Sport and Exercise 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1469029225000895)), one longitudinal study links app use in adolescence with more disordered eating in early adulthood ([PMC12547374](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12547374/)), and a 2024 trial for adolescents with obesity deliberately avoided calorie counting because of eating-disorder risk ([PMC11650080](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11650080/)). Causation is unproven, but the pattern linked to harm is a personal diary with goals, deficits and streaks. A full clone moves Coolinary towards that pattern and away from its "the whole family eats lighter together" design, the design its own plan built in response to the 2019 backlash against Kurbo, a weight-loss app for children ([IAEDP](https://edr.iaedpfoundation.com/update-new-weight-watchers-anti-obesity-app-for-children-draws-criticisms/)).

The case for building deserves an answer. A diary creates a daily habit, and the business model names "the daily habit" as part of Coolinary's defence, but the plan plus an "I ate this" tap already gives a daily touchpoint. Trackers will not accept Coolinary's meals, so one-tap logging is only possible in-app, but the lite version delivers exactly that for about a quarter of the clone's effort. MacroFactor and Cal AI show small teams can win, but each was a team of two or three doing only tracking, aimed at English-speaking markets, and neither had to beat a 240,000-payer local leader at home. Strategy also favours staying complementary. Fitatu is profitable, newly controlled by investors, focused on Germany, Austria and Switzerland, and open about acquisitions in Europe ([XYZ.pl](https://xyz.pl/tworcy-sympatii-oddali-kontrole-w-fitatu-grupa-znanych-inwestorow-rozkreci-zywieniowa-aplikacje-w-europie-zachodniej/)). A clone would make Coolinary a head-on competitor of a funded company, while a complementary planner remains a plausible partner, co-marketing ally or acquisition.

The work starts in Phase 0, before anything is built. The 15–20 interviews and the concierge test should ask which tracker each household member uses and whether they pay for it, because no survey exists on how many family cooks already track, and that single number decides what the health-store link is worth. A Fitatu Premium subscription on one iPhone and one Android phone will settle the open technical questions: which data types Fitatu writes to the health store and in how much detail, whether it ignores nutrition written by other apps, and whether its deep links accept text to pre-fill a search. In parallel, the founder should ask FatSecret for Premier Free terms with Polish and German data, test whether API-created entries appear in the consumer app, and send Fitatu one e-mail presenting Coolinary as a household planner that sends users into Fitatu.

Release 1 should show each dish's per-person ingredient list in grams with a "copy for your tracker" button and publish recipe pages with schema.org markup. Release 2, which brings personal and family calorie goals, should add the health-store link: opt-in and for the phone owner only, with eaten calories subtracted from the target on the phone, a "my tracker" setting to stop double counting, a manual "already eaten" fallback for free Fitatu users, and an "I ate this (my portion)" write for users who do not use a tracker. That "I ate this" step is the second item on the lite tracker's build list, so the link is the lite tracker's first slice and no work is wasted if the lite version follows.

The lite log should be built only if launch data show both that most adults who set a goal have no tracker writing to their health store, and that those adults tap "I ate this" on most planned meals while off-plan logging ranks among the top feedback requests; these thresholds are my proposal and should be tuned once real numbers arrive. The research team's own triggers for moving from lite to a fuller tracker are more than 30% of logged entries being off-plan, a search-miss rate above 15% in Poland, and weight features entering the top three requests, and any database-heavy feature should start in Germany. A full clone makes sense only if Coolinary deliberately decides to become a calorie-tracking company.

## Conclusion

The most accurate food-log entry anyone can make is a planned, cooked meal with a known recipe and grams; giving an AI the ingredient list cuts its error by more than half compared with a photo, and only Coolinary holds that recipe. Coolinary's advantage therefore sits *upstream* of the diary: it decides what the household eats, and trackers are downstream record-keepers. Because reading from the health stores is open while writing into trackers is closed, Coolinary's integration value will grow with Apple Health and Health Connect, especially if Apple ships its own food diary, rather than with deals with individual trackers.

Coolinary's leverage with Fitatu or Yazio will come from traction, not code. Both sell distribution deals, not data access, and they will listen to a planner that brings them engaged users. Staying complementary keeps open the outcome this market has rewarded so far, trackers buying planners, while a clone would close it and spend most of a solo founder's first year building a weaker copy of a product two well-funded companies already make.
